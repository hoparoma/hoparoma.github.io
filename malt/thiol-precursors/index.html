Malt thiol precursors: which grains carry them

Hops are not the only source of tropical thiols. Pale barley malt carries bound 3MH precursors of its own, and a thiol-releasing yeast can free them even in a wort that is barely hopped. Kilning and roasting destroy them, and non-barley grains carry little. This table rates every malt in Hoparoma's library.

What the literature measured

  • Pale barley malts contain glutathionylated 3-mercaptohexanol precursor in the low hundreds of µg/kg range, and some special malts carry comparable or higher amounts; wheat is a poor carrier Chenot et al. 2023.
  • Thiol groups in malt protein fall as colour rises and are undetectable in the darkest roasted malts, which is why roast and heavily caramelised malts rate negligible here Fleischer & Hellwig 2022.
  • A genetically modified yeast fermenting an all-barley wort with only a light Cascade addition produced free 3MH well above its sensory threshold, whereas wheat, rice, and sorghum worts carried far less precursor Molitor et al. 2022.
  • Malt and hops both supply thiol S-conjugates, and a lager yeast can release thiols from them during fermentation; disulfide-bonded thiols in malt and hops are a further precursor pool Chenot et al. 2022 Kishimoto et al. 2022.

The levels below are a relative ranking, not a molar yield. Hoparoma uses them as one input to its thiol model; the resulting aroma is a research-based estimate, and the perceived effect of malt precursors in a heavily hopped beer is still an open question in the literature.

The scale

  • High Reserved for specialty base malts documented as unusually precursor-rich. None of the library's standard malts qualify yet.
  • Medium Standard pale barley base malts and lightly kilned specialty malts.
  • Low Light roast or light caramelisation; precursors reduced.
  • Negligible Fully roasted or heavily caramelised; precursors effectively gone.
  • Low (non-barley) Wheat, rye, and oat products; barley-type precursors are scarce.
