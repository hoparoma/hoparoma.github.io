Aroma in one line

Brewers describe Cascade as grapefruit floral pine. In Hoparoma's hop notes its thiol character is filed as classic citrus. Part of its fruit character comes from bound thiol precursors; the rest rides on the oils listed below.

Thiol potential

3MH family (passionfruit, grapefruit) Significant
4MMP (blackcurrant, catty) Significant

Most of a hop's thiol aroma sits in the cone as odourless precursors bound to cysteine or glutathione; free thiols are a small fraction of the pool Gros et al. 2012 Kishimoto 2009. The tiers above summarise published precursor measurements and supplier statements for this variety. They are qualitative on purpose: Hoparoma's internal weighting is not published, and lot-to-lot variation is large.

Variety note: Terroir dominates crop year signal; PNW vs NY Cascade bound 3MH differs by about 3x.

Which yeast brings it out

A medium or high β-lyase strain, such as the London III family, LalBrew Pomona, or any of the thiol-forward strains, brings out the bound fraction. A clean Chico-type strain leaves it mostly bound, which is fine if you want the oil-driven character alone.

Rule of thumb only. See the yeast table for every strain's rating and the literature behind it.
